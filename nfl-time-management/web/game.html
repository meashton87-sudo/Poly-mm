How to play

Three real late-game spots a day, from real NFL games. You call every play for one team until the game ends: the run or pass, where it goes, the tempo, timeouts, spikes, kneels, field goals and punts. Toggle Get out of bounds to try to stop the clock (it raises the odds; it never guarantees it).

Each play's outcome is drawn from the 160,000 real NFL snaps of 2022-26, labeled with the call behind them: outside, off-tackle and inside runs to the strong or weak side, QB sneaks and designed runs, RPOs, quick and deep passes to either side or the middle, screens, play-action, rollouts, trick plays and the Hail Mary. Anything that happened on those snaps can happen to you: sacks, strip-sacks, picks and pick-sixes, fumbles, bad snaps, drops, throwaways, scrambles, flags, and runners who can't get out of bounds. Outcomes match the down, distance, field position and game situation, adjusted for how good these two teams are at that play. The percentiles on each call rank your offense, and their defense, against the league on that exact play.

Strong side means the wide side of the field and weak side the short side: public charting doesn't record where the tight end lines up, but it does record which hash the ball is on, and offenses usually set their strength to the field.

After every decision the simulator plays the rest of the game thousands of times for each option. Your score is the win probability your calls add over a typical NFL play-caller in the same spots, and the percentile says how many play-callers you beat.
